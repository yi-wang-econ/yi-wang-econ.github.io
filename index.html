Job Market Paper

CBDC, Financial Disintermediation and Financial Fragility

Paper (PDF)
Abstract

A central bank digital currency (CBDC) competes with bank deposits as a means of payment. This paper studies a limit on the amount of CBDC a household may hold, in a model that places banks with an endogenous leverage constraint in the centralized market of a New Monetarist economy. Households pay with cash, deposits or CBDC in decentralized meetings that differ in what sellers accept. Deposits are a cheap source of funds for banks because they are used for payments, and the holding limit determines how much of that advantage CBDC takes away.

In a calibration to the United States, CBDC substitutes closely for deposits and shrinks bank lending, the capital stock and consumption. The same mechanism makes banks safer: dearer funding forces them to delever, lowering the probability of insolvency. Bank runs become slightly harder to trigger as the limit rises, up to the limit at which deposits stop carrying a liquidity premium; above it they become easier to trigger, because the CBDC that households hold slows the recovery of banks after a run.

The Macroeconomic Effects of Overwork and Health

Master's thesis, Tufts University, 2021

A search-and-matching model with health in which workers whose productivity falls below a threshold choose to work overtime to make up for it. The model is used to study the aggregate effects of overwork and how overwork and health interact.
